The story of suya

Born on the streets of Northern Nigeria

Suya started with the Hausa people of Northern Nigeria, where the mai suya (the suya man) fires up his grill as the sun goes down. Thin strips of beef go onto wooden skewers, get coated in yaji, and cook slowly over open flame until the edges char and the spice turns into a smoky, peppery crust.

Yaji is what makes it suya. It's a blend of roasted ground peanuts, ginger, chili pepper and spices, and every suya man guards his own recipe. Across Nigeria you'll find a line at the good stands late into the night. People eat it hot off the grill, wrapped in paper with sliced onions and tomatoes, at parties, after work, or as an excuse to stay out a little longer.

We make our beef suya the traditional way, with our own yaji, so you can have that taste at home or bring it to your next event, wherever you are.

Questions

Why only Saturday to Tuesday?

Taking orders early in the week gives us time to grill, freeze overnight and ship during the week, so your suya doesn't sit in transit over the weekend.

Does it contain peanuts?

Yes. Traditional yaji spice is made with ground peanuts. If you or a guest has a peanut allergy, please tell us when you order.

How spicy is it?

Traditional suya has a peppery kick. Tell us mild, medium or hot when you order and we'll adjust the yaji.

Where do you ship from?

Our office is in Plainfield, Illinois, and orders ship frozen from our kitchen in Texas.

How much is shipping?

Shipping depends on where you are and how many packs you order. We'll give you the exact total when we confirm your order by phone.

How do I pay?

We'll go over payment options when we confirm your order by phone.

Why is it frozen?

We grill your suya fresh, then freeze it overnight so it stays fresh in transit. Put it in the freezer when it arrives, or the fridge if you're eating it within a day or two.

How should I reheat it?

Thaw in the fridge overnight, then warm in the oven at 350°F for 8–10 minutes or in an air fryer for 4–5 minutes. Avoid the microwave if you can — it softens the crust.

What is suya?

Suya (also called tsire) is a traditional Hausa smoke-grilled spiced meat on a skewer from Northern Nigeria, coated in yaji — a blend of ground peanuts, ginger, chili and spices.
